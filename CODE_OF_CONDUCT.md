# Tag Party Code of Conduct

Tag Party is an open-source game project built around competition, experimentation,
and community contribution.

We want project spaces to remain welcoming, constructive, and useful for people
who contribute code, design, maps, UI, documentation, testing, feedback, or other
work.

Competitive banter and disagreement are expected around a game project.
Harassment, threats, targeted abuse, and attacks on contributors are not.

## Expected Behavior

Participants are expected to:

- treat other contributors and community members with respect;
- discuss ideas, implementations, and gameplay decisions rather than attacking
  the people proposing them;
- give and receive constructive technical and design criticism in good faith;
- respect differences in experience, background, perspective, and communication
  style;
- avoid deliberately disruptive behavior, spam, or repeated bad-faith arguments;
- respect the privacy and personal information of other participants;
- follow reasonable instructions from project maintainers when moderation is
  required.

Strong disagreement is allowed.

Criticizing a design, implementation, balance decision, pull request, or technical
approach is allowed.

Personal abuse is not.

## Unacceptable Behavior

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- harassment, intimidation, stalking, or threats;
- discriminatory or degrading comments directed at another person;
- targeted insults or sustained personal attacks;
- unwanted sexual attention or sexualized harassment;
- publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information
  without permission;
- impersonating project maintainers or falsely presenting unofficial material as
  an official Tag Party communication;
- deliberately disrupting project discussions, issue tracking, pull requests,
  playtests, or other official community spaces;
- repeated bad-faith behavior after a maintainer has asked for it to stop.

Ordinary disagreement, criticism, competitive discussion, and good-faith mistakes
are not automatically violations of this Code of Conduct.

Context and patterns of behavior matter.

## Project Spaces

This Code of Conduct applies to official Tag Party project spaces, including:

- the Tag Party GitHub repository;
- issues, pull requests, code reviews, and repository discussions;
- official Tag Party community channels;
- official playtests and project-organized events;
- other spaces explicitly operated by the Tag Party project.

It also applies when someone is acting as an authorized representative of
Tag Party, such as through an official project account or at an official project
event.

This Code of Conduct is not intended to govern unrelated activity conducted by
community members in their personal capacity outside Tag Party project spaces.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers may take reasonable action when behavior violates this Code
of Conduct.

Depending on the circumstances, actions may include:

- asking a participant to stop specific behavior;
- editing, hiding, or removing inappropriate project content;
- closing or locking disruptive discussions;
- rejecting or closing contributions;
- temporarily restricting participation;
- permanently banning a participant from official project spaces.

Enforcement should be proportionate to the severity, frequency, and context of
the behavior.

Serious conduct, including credible threats, targeted harassment, doxxing, or
other behavior that creates an immediate safety concern, may result in immediate
removal without prior warning.

## Reporting

If you experience or observe conduct that may violate this Code of Conduct,
report it privately to a project maintainer through an official private contact
method published by the Tag Party project.

Do not include sensitive personal information in a public issue.

Reports should include, where practical:

- what happened;
- where it happened;
- relevant links, screenshots, or other context;
- whether the behavior is ongoing.

Reports will be reviewed in good faith and handled with reasonable respect for
the privacy of the people involved.

The project cannot promise absolute confidentiality where disclosure is required
for safety, legal compliance, or proper investigation, but unnecessary disclosure
of a reporter's identity should be avoided.

## Maintainer Discretion

No Code of Conduct can anticipate every situation.

Project maintainers may use reasonable judgment when interpreting and enforcing
these rules, with the goal of protecting productive participation and the health
of the project rather than policing ordinary disagreement.

Moderation decisions do not create an obligation to merge, accept, preserve, or
continue hosting any contribution or discussion.
